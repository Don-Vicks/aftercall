# Privacy: what leaves the Mac, and what anyone keeps

Aftercall's rule: meeting content is processed on the Mac or inside NEAR AI Cloud's secure hardware, and Aftercall's own servers keep nothing about a meeting after a response returns. This page lists every place data goes, what each place keeps and for how long, and the code that enforces it. Line numbers are for this commit.

For the follow-ups in one table (what each sends, who approves it, and what record it leaves), see [Follow-ups: what leaves, who approves, what's kept](#follow-ups-what-leaves-who-approves-whats-kept) below.

| Your taps on a paired phone (approve, dismiss, undo autopilot) | Your Mac, through the relay, sealed and padded to one size | Backend: until your Mac collects them, a day at most |
| A NEAR Intents payment | 1Click API (`1click.chaindefuser.com`) | 1Click's policy: amount, destination address, your refund account |
| A recovery phrase you keep other than on paper (Copy, Save kit, or Print or save as PDF on a phrase screen) | Your clipboard for up to 60 s; a recovery kit file only where you choose in the Save dialog; or your printer, or a PDF wherever you save it | Not encrypted, and Delete everything can't reach a saved kit or printout. The clipboard is cleared after 60 s, or when you leave the step, if it still holds the words |
| A meeting you export (Export → Copy as Markdown, or Print or save as PDF) | Your clipboard, then wherever you paste it; or your printer, or a PDF wherever you save it | Not encrypted, and Delete everything can't reach it. Your notes and the transcript only when you tick them; never email addresses, NEAR accounts, transaction ids or proof details |

## Follow-ups: what leaves, who approves, what's kept

A follow-up has to leave the secure hardware to do anything. This is what each one shares, who can say yes, and what record it leaves. The app says the same in place: a "What leaves?" line on each follow-up card (`packages/core/src/followups/boundaries.ts`, tested) and a "Where this meeting went" trail on each meeting.

| Follow-up | What leaves, and to whom | Who approves | What's kept, and where |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Recap, **Draft in Mail** (the default) | The summary, decisions and open action items, to your own mail app. Nothing goes to Aftercall's servers | You, when you send it from Mail | Your mail provider. On the Mac: when you drafted it |
| Recap, **Send through Aftercall** | The same recap, to Aftercall's backend (in memory for one request), then Resend, then the recipient | You tap Send, with a 5-second undo. Autopilot sends only to team domains you list, with a 60-second undo | Resend keeps sent mail under its own policy; the backend keeps a count. On the Mac: the time sent and Resend's email id |
| Recap, **Post to Slack** (only with a Slack webhook connected) | The same recap text (summary, decisions, open action items and the footer) as a Slack message, from the Mac to Slack, into the channel the webhook was made for. Nothing goes to Aftercall's servers; the transcript and the audio don't go | You tap Post to Slack, with a 5-second undo. Autopilot, the paired phone and coding agents can't post | Slack keeps the post in that channel under your workspace's settings. On the Mac: the time posted |
| **Payment within your caps** | The payee's NEAR account and the amount, to NEAR, with a fixed memo ("Aftercall payment") and an id made of the recording's start time and the follow-up's number. No meeting title or text | You tap Approve. Autopilot pays only listed payees within your per-payment cap and today's allowance, with a 60-second undo. The contract checks the payee list and both caps either way | On NEAR, publicly and for good. On the Mac: the transaction hash |
| **Payment over a cap** | The same, parked on the contract instead of paid | Only your own NEAR wallet, on the `/sign` page, can approve or decline it. The app's key can't | On NEAR: the parked payment and your decision |
| **Payment on Solana or Base** (NEAR Intents 1Click) | The amount, the payee's address and your refund account, to the 1Click API; then the payment, to NEAR, with 1Click's deposit address and quote id | As above, and only for payees you opted in, under a cross-chain cap that is 0 (off) until your wallet sets it. Over that cap it's refused, not parked | 1Click's own policy; NEAR as above |
| **Deal held in escrow** (Handshake; on testnet, not yet run live) | To NEAR: the payee's account, the amount, the follow-up id, the meeting's proof id and hash, whether it has a condition, and a salted hash of the terms, never the terms themselves | You tap Ask to confirm; the contract holds it within your caps, or parks it over one for your own wallet. Only the payee's own NEAR wallet confirms it. Release is your tap (never autopilot, never the phone); without a condition, the payee's confirmation pays them. Only your wallet takes it back, and only before they confirm or 30 days after | On NEAR, publicly and for good: the escrow and its state. On the Mac: the confirm link, the transactions and the state as last read |
| **Confirm link** for that deal | Copied by you, it goes wherever you paste it. The link's `#fragment` carries the terms: the amount and the words heard, the condition and both exact quotes from the call, the meeting's title, the payee's name as you wrote it, your NEAR account and the meeting's proof hash. Browsers never send a fragment to a server; `/deal` reads it in the payee's browser and reads the escrow from NEAR. **Emailed through Aftercall**, the link (fragment included) passes Aftercall's backend in memory for one request and Resend, like a recap, under the same 50-a-day limit | You choose to copy or email it | Backend: nothing but the count. Resend: sent mail under its own policy. Whoever has the link can read the terms, as with a forwarded recap |
| **Claim link** for a new payee | A token hash and the amount, to Aftercall's backend. The payee's name and the meeting title stay in the link itself | The payee chooses where to be paid; you confirm them as a payee in your own wallet | Backend: the token hash, owner, amount, expiry, status and the account the payee shared |
| **Changing your caps, payees or pause** | A transaction from your wallet, to NEAR | Only your wallet. Each change needs 1 yoctoNEAR attached, which the app's limited key can't attach | On NEAR |
| **Coding agents** (Claude Code, Codex) | To Aftercall's servers, nothing: agents read meetings over a socket only the running app opens, and only in the projects you let them reach. What an agent reads, it sends to its own model provider, as with anything it reads | You. Agents are off by default; claiming, completing and proposing follow-ups is a second switch, also off. Agents can never send, pay or change your rules | On the Mac: a sealed list of each read, claim, completion, proposal and refusal (the newest 200), never meeting content |
| **A follow-up a coding agent proposes** (`draft_follow_up`) | Nothing until you approve it; then the same as the recap or payment rows above | You tap Approve on the Mac. It shows "Proposed by" the agent, with its reason in place of a quote; it never runs on autopilot and never reaches the phone. A payment only to a payee already on your list, within your per-payment cap and what's left today | On the Mac: the proposal, with the agent's name and reason, in the meeting's sealed `notes.json` (at most 20 per meeting); the activity list records it |
| **Approving from your phone** | Each waiting recap's address and subject; each payment's payee, amount, heard words, route and quote; the meeting's title and start. Sealed to a key only your Mac and phone hold, through Aftercall's relay; a held payment's wallet links also carry your NEAR account and what's left of today's cap. Your taps come back the same way | You, on the phone, with a 5-second undo. The Mac checks each tap again and runs its own button under the same rules and caps. A held payment opens your NEAR wallet instead | Relay: the latest sealed snapshot and hashes of the tokens, until you unpair or 30 days after your Mac last called; taps until the Mac collects them, a day at most. Phone: the pairing and the latest snapshot in the browser's storage, until you unpair |

Every meeting also leaves a proof on NEAR, for good: the recording's start time, a hash and the model name, never the meeting itself. The proof link you share carries the checks, hashes and counts, your logged notice and your NEAR account, never the notes or the transcript. Proofs made from 7 Nov also hold a Merkle root over the transcript's lines and how many lines there are; a line link adds the one line you chose ([Sharing one line](#sharing-one-line)). Everything else, field by field with file and line pointers, is below.

## On the Mac

- Audio is written by the recorder as `mic-NNN.wav` (you) and `system-NNN.wav` (the others) in the app's data folder, `recordings/<id>/`.
- Only files inside that folder can be uploaded, and only `.wav`: `apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/nearai.rs:153` (`check_audio_path`).
- Right after the notes are saved, before the proof goes to NEAR, the audio and the cached chunk transcripts are deleted: `apps/desktop/src/RecordingScreen.tsx:222` calls `delete_audio`, `apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/lib.rs:860`. The processing page shows the steps in that order: "Audio deleted from this Mac" comes before "Proof recorded on NEAR". If the app quits before that, or the delete fails, `sweep_audio` (`lib.rs:884`, run at launch) deletes the audio of every recording that has notes; recordings without notes keep theirs so they can be processed or retried (test `audio_left_beside_saved_notes_is_swept_and_unprocessed_audio_stays`).
- The notice: ticking "I've told everyone on the call" records the time you ticked it, and how you told them if you pick one (`apps/desktop/src/recording/RecordSheet.tsx`). It is saved in `meeting.json` with the recording (`NoticeLog`, `lib.rs:125`, which accepts only `by: "you"` and the known ways), and copied into the proof as your statement (`ProcessInput.notice`, `packages/core/src/notes/pipeline.ts`). The proof hash covers it; nothing checks it. Samples get none.
- Editing an action item (text, owner or due date) changes only the meeting's copy in `notes.json`, sealed like the rest, under the same file lock as ticks and agent claims (`edit_item`, `apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/notes_file.rs:193`). The model's signed notes and the proof hash never change; Restore original puts back the signed item. A recap marks each open edited item "(edited by the sender)" and its footer says Aftercall didn't check those changes (`packages/core/src/followups/recap.ts`).
- Recovery phrases (a new testnet account's and Aftercall's own) are shown once on the phrase screens (`apps/desktop/src/RecoveryPhrase.tsx`). Besides writing them down you can Copy, Save kit or Print (`apps/desktop/src/recoveryKit.ts`). Copy puts the 12 words on the clipboard, marked concealed so clipboard managers are asked not to keep them, and, after 60 seconds or when you leave the step, reads it back and clears it only if it still holds exactly those words, so something you copied since stays. A clipboard app that ignores the concealed mark, or Universal Clipboard, may still keep a copy. Save kit opens the macOS Save dialog; `recovery_save_kit` (`apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/recovery_kit.rs`) writes the kit to the path you picked with 0600 permissions, refuses Aftercall's own data, config, cache and log folders and links, and never logs the text. Print mounts the same kit as a print-only page with the rest of the window kept off the paper, and takes it out of the page again at your first click or key press after the print panel closes, or when you leave the step. No copy goes to a temp file, cache, the app's data or logs, and a new account's phrase is still forgotten when you continue.
- Export (the meeting page's share button, `apps/desktop/src/ExportMenu.tsx`) copies the meeting as Markdown or opens the print panel, where macOS can save a PDF. Both read `meetingExport` (`packages/core/src/export.ts`): title, date, project, people, summary, decisions, action items (edited ones marked "(edited by you)"), follow-ups by name, amount and status, and whether the notes were verified and where to see the checks. Your notes and the transcript go in only when you tick them in the menu, and the ticks reset each time the page opens. Email addresses, NEAR accounts, transaction ids, claim links and proof details stay out; someone the calendar has no name for is counted ("You, Ada and 1 other") and their transcript lines read "Others". The menu says that copying uses the clipboard, and that copies and PDFs aren't encrypted and Delete everything can't remove them. Nothing is sent anywhere.
- The estimated cost of a run (tokens and Whisper seconds NEAR AI reported, times its public prices on 1 Nov 2026, `packages/core/src/cost.ts`) is saved in `notes.json`, not in the proof.
- The NEAR AI key lives in the macOS Keychain and never reaches the web view: `apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/nearai.rs:43`.
- The Slack webhook (Settings → Slack) lives in the Keychain as `slack-webhook` and never goes back to the web view: the window can only ask whether one is set, save one, remove it (Disconnect) or post a recap through it (`apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/slack.rs`). Only a URL starting `https://hooks.slack.com/services/` followed by three parts of letters and digits is accepted, checked in the window (`slackWebhookProblem`, `packages/core/src/followups/slack.ts`) and again in Rust before it is saved and before every post (`check_webhook_url`). The post goes from Rust with a 15-second timeout, follows no redirects, turns link previews off, and its errors never contain the URL (tests in `slack.rs`, against a local server). Meeting text has `&`, `<` and `>` escaped, so a recap can't ping the channel or mention anyone (`slackRecapText`, `slack.test.ts`).
- Calendar lookups (EventKit), search, the People list, the MCP server for agents (shipped inside the app as `mcp/aftercall-mcp.mjs` and run with your own `node`) and backups are all local.
- To warn when your mic or the call goes silent, and to ask "Call ended?", the recorder measures each side's loudness over each second (`apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/recorder.rs`). The levels aren't saved. A warning notification goes out only when Aftercall's window isn't focused, and says only which side went silent and that recording goes on (`warningText`, `apps/desktop/src/recording/notify.tsx`).
- **Delete everything on this Mac** (Settings → Privacy and your data) removes the app's data folder (with the agent settings, the agent activity list and a paired phone's `phone.json`) and its six Keychain items (`data-key`, `near-app-key`, `nearai-api-key`, `backup-key`, `phone-relay`, `slack-webhook` under `dev.aftercall.app`), after you type "delete": `apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/wipe.rs`, called by `delete_all_data` in `lib.rs`. The folder goes first and the keys only once it's gone. A paired phone is then unpaired on the relay as well (`deleteEverything`, `apps/desktop/src/library.ts`). Proofs and payments on NEAR, this Mac's access key on your NEAR account, the agent wallet's balance, backups you exported and meetings you copied or printed stay.

## NEAR AI Cloud

- The only AI endpoint is `https://cloud-api.near.ai/v1` (`apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/nearai.rs:25`), and only the Confidential TEE models in `packages/core/src/notes/pipeline.ts:17-18` (`WHISPER_MODEL`, `SUMMARY_MODEL`).
- Every call is capped: the notes request at 12,288 output tokens (`SUMMARY_MAX_TOKENS`, `pipeline.ts:31`) and Ask at 6,144 (`ASK_MAX_TOKENS`, `packages/core/src/ask.ts:33`). A reply cut off at the cap stops the run before anything is parsed, saved or shown.
- What is sent: each audio chunk (with project and payee names, and the project brief's words to expect, as Whisper's spelling prompt: `whisperVocabulary`, `packages/core/src/notes/pipeline.ts`), then the merged transcript with the project name, the payee list, the project brief and your notes if there are any (`summaryRequestText`, `packages/core/src/notes/pipeline.ts`). The transcript is end-to-end encrypted to a verified summary node's attested key, or not sent: it never goes over TLS instead. If no verified node offers an attested key, nothing is sent at all, audio included (`requireAttested`, `packages/core/src/notes/pipeline.ts`). When NEAR AI answers that the attested model key is no longer one it serves (`e2eeStaleKey`, `packages/core/src/errors.ts`), the app checks the summary hardware again and retries end-to-end encrypted once, to a key from the new quotes; if that fails, it stops. Any other error, a refusal of the encryption headers included, stops processing, and the transcript isn't sent again. Proofs made by earlier versions, which could send the transcript over TLS, still say so. Payee and project names come from your agent wallet on NEAR; the sample payee is used only for sample recordings.
- **Ask your meetings** (`packages/core/src/ask.ts`): the question is searched on the Mac first. Only the transcript lines that match (at most 8, each cut to 400 characters, each labelled with its meeting's title, time and speaker: "You", the one person the calendar invited, or "Others"), today's date, the question and the briefs of the projects those lines come from (about, people and words to expect, not what to watch for; `askBriefBlock`) go to the Qwen TEE, end-to-end encrypted to a model key that passed the attestation checks moments before. If no line matches, or the gateway or model check fails, nothing is sent; there is no unencrypted fallback. Quotes in the answer that aren't in those lines are dropped.
- The notes prompt asks the model to write the title, summary, decisions and action items in English whatever language was spoken, and to copy evidence quotes and the words said about amounts in the language spoken, never translated (`NOTES_SYSTEM_PROMPT`, `packages/core/src/notes/schema.ts:114`). The quotes are then checked against the transcript on the Mac, so a translated quote is dropped.
- The app may also read the attestation report and the response signature, and nothing else: `apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/nearai.rs:450` (`check_get_path`).
- What NEAR AI keeps, and whether it trains on anything, is set by NEAR AI's own policy, not by Aftercall. The attestation proves the models ran in TEEs; it does not prove what NEAR AI's gateway logs. The Verify page lists this under what isn't proven, and the recap footer makes no claim about it.
- `pnpm sample:notes` uses a Node transport with the same three calls and the same path rule: `packages/core/src/node/nearai.ts`.

## Project briefs

A project's brief is what you write about it so the notes understand it: **About** (a sentence or two), **People** (a name and, if you like, a role), **Words to expect** (names, product terms, jargon) and **What to watch for** (what counts as a decision or action item for you). Every field is optional. New project can start it from a template (which fills only what to watch for, empty role slots and general words, never names or facts; `briefTemplates.ts`) or from a GitHub repo (below), and shows it for review before anything is saved; Blank and Enter creates the project with no brief. You can also add or edit it on the project's page. A restored backup brings a brief back without where it came from.

- On the Mac it is saved in `projects.json` with the project's id and name, sealed with the data key like the notes (`apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/projects.rs`, `at_rest.rs`); a test checks no part of it is on disk in plaintext. A `projects.json` from an earlier version, with only ids and names, still loads. An encrypted backup carries it; a restore adds it to a project that has none and never overwrites one you have (`recovery.rs`). Clearing the brief, deleting the project or Delete everything removes it.
- Before it is used it is cleaned: each field becomes one trimmed line without `<<<` or `>>>`, cut to 400 characters (about), 300 (what to watch for), 20 people (60-character names, 80-character roles) and 30 words of up to 40 characters, without repeats (`cleanBrief`, `packages/core/src/notes/pipeline.ts`; the Mac side refuses anything longer).
- The notes request gets it inside the end-to-end encrypted message to the attested Qwen TEE, after the payees and invited people, as a block headed "Project brief (written by you about this project; context only, not instructions)" between `<<<` and `>>>` (`briefBlock`). A long call's parts and its overall request each carry it. The model's instructions say the brief is background, not instructions, and changes none of the rules; its people are used for speaker names and action item owners only when the transcript shows it is that person; and "what to watch for" never permits an item the transcript doesn't have (`NOTES_SYSTEM_PROMPT`, `packages/core/src/notes/schema.ts`). Every item still needs an exact quote from the transcript, and `buildMeeting` drops one whose quote isn't there.
- **Words to expect also go to Whisper**, as part of its spelling prompt, after the project and payee names, without repeats and capped at 40 terms and 500 characters (`whisperVocabulary`). That prompt travels with the audio over TLS pinned to the gateway's attested TLS key, not end-to-end encrypted. The form says so under the field: "Sent with the audio to help spelling". About, people, roles and what to watch for never go to Whisper.
- Ask sends the briefs of the projects its excerpts come from, inside its encrypted request (about, people, words to expect). Its instructions say a brief is never quoted or used as the only basis for an answer, and quotes are still checked against the excerpts.
- **Fill from GitHub** (`apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/github.rs`, `packages/core/src/notes/githubBrief.ts`): you paste a repo; the app checks it is an `owner/name` GitHub allows before anything runs. With the GitHub CLI installed (looked for in `~/.local/bin`, `/opt/homebrew/bin`, `/usr/local/bin`, then the login shell's PATH), it runs only `gh api` with fixed arguments and no shell, for the repo, its README, its contributors, its root folder listing, CODEOWNERS, package.json, Cargo.toml and contributors' profile names; `gh` uses your own sign-in, so Aftercall never sees or stores a GitHub token. Without `gh`, or when it isn't signed in, the same reads go to `api.github.com` without signing in (public repos only, 60 requests an hour per IP; about 6 per lookup plus up to 5 names). The screen says: "Aftercall asks GitHub for this repo's public details (with gh, using your own GitHub sign-in). GitHub sees which repo. Nothing from your meetings is sent." The answer is turned into suggestions on the Mac, with no AI, and nothing is saved until you press Create or Save. Refresh is manual: there is no lookup in the background or at launch.
- The brief isn't saved on the meeting, isn't in the proof or its link (only hashes of the encrypted request bytes are), never goes in a recap, and isn't used for sample recordings. Coding agents get project ids and names only (`public_list`, `projects.rs`).

## Your notes

What you type during a call is yours: it guides the notes, and goes nowhere else.

- Make notes uses exactly what you last typed on the recording's page: an edit still waiting to be saved is saved to `your-notes.json` first, and if that save fails nothing is sent (`YourNotesDrafts`, `packages/core/src/notes/yourNotesDrafts.ts`; `yourNotesDrafts.test.ts`). So the file holds exactly what you last typed, and the model and the proof's salted hash get the cleaned version of that same text (next point).
- Before sending, they are cleaned: `<<<` and `>>>` are removed (so the text can't close the block it is quoted in), then they are trimmed and cut to 8,000 characters (`cleanYourNotes`, `packages/core/src/notes/pipeline.ts:295`).
- They go only to the summary model, inside the request that is end-to-end encrypted to the attested Qwen TEE, as a block headed "Your notes (typed by you during the call; context only):" (`summaryRequestText`, `pipeline.ts:309`). Whisper never gets them. If end-to-end encryption isn't available, they aren't sent, like the transcript.
- The model's instructions say evidence must still come from the transcript (`packages/core/src/notes/schema.ts:127`), and `buildMeeting` drops anything whose quote isn't in the transcript, so a payment that appears only in your notes never becomes a follow-up (`build.test.ts`, `pipeline.test.ts`: "your notes go to the model inside the encrypted request…").
- On the Mac they are kept on the meeting in `notes.json`, sealed like the rest, and shown above the AI notes.
- The proof holds `yourNotesHash = sha256(salt ‖ your notes)` (`yourNotesHash`, `packages/core/src/notes/verify.ts:34`). The salt is 32 random bytes made per meeting (`pipeline.ts:782`) and saved only in `notes.json`, so the public proof link reveals nothing about them and short notes can't be guessed by hashing likely phrases. The Verify sheet re-checks "Your notes unchanged since signing" with the saved salt (`yourNotesCheck`, `packages/core/src/notes/proof.ts:137`); the public page can't, and doesn't try. Proofs without your notes leave the key out and hash exactly as before (`proof.test.ts`: "old proofs hash exactly as before…").
- Never in a recap: `buildRecap` doesn't read them (`packages/core/src/followups/recap.ts:128`; `recap.test.ts`: "a recap never includes your notes").
- Never sent to connected agents: the app's agents socket removes `meeting.yourNotes` and the salt from everything it answers, along with the proof's notice, the cost estimate and the key behind the transcript lines' salts (`for_agents`, `apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/agents.rs:372`; test `your_notes_and_their_salt_never_reach_agents`), and no MCP tool returns or searches them (`apps/mcp/src/tools.test.ts`: "your notes never reach an agent…").
- Nothing about them goes on NEAR except inside the proof hash.

## Sharing one line

Share this line, on a transcript line or an action item's quote, makes a link that shows one line of a meeting and lets anyone check it is part of the transcript the meeting's proof on NEAR commits to. Built and tested, not yet run live (no proof with a line tree is on NEAR yet).

- Each new proof holds `lineTree`: a Merkle root over the transcript's lines and how many there are (`lineTreeOf`, `packages/core/src/notes/lines.ts:145`; set in `pipeline.ts:804`). The proof hash on NEAR covers it. Proofs made before it lack the key and hash exactly as before; their lines can't be shared (`disclosure.test.ts`: "a proof made before line trees hashes and checks exactly as before").
- A leaf commits to the line's index, its time, its side (`you` or `others`), its words and a salt. Each line's salt is HMAC-SHA256 of its index under a 32-byte random key made per meeting (`lineSalt`, `lines.ts:67`; `lineKey`, `pipeline.ts:783`), saved only in `notes.json`, sealed like the rest. Without a line's salt, its leaf can't be matched by hashing likely words, and one line's salt says nothing about another's.
- The link (`/verify#line=…`, `encodeLineDisclosure`, `packages/core/src/notes/disclosure.ts:20`) carries what a proof link carries (your NEAR account, the contract, the proof bundle with its checks, hashes and counts, your logged notice) plus the line's index, time, side, words, salt and Merkle path. From the proof it also follows when the recording started (the meeting id is its start time) and how many lines the transcript has. It never carries the title, another line, the notes, your notes, the key or a name: a line from the call's side says only that, even when your calendar names who was on the other side.
- The sheet says this before it copies anything, and the app makes the link only after the line checks against the proof on this Mac (`ShareLineSheet`, `apps/desktop/src/ShareLineSheet.tsx`). It is offered only for a meeting whose proof is on NEAR, has a line tree, and whose key is in `notes.json`.
- Whoever gets the link can read the line and forward it; Aftercall can't take it back. The proof on NEAR stays a hash.
- Like every proof link, it also carries `transcriptHash` and `notesHash`: plain sha256, with no salt, of the whole transcript and the whole notes (`pipeline.ts:799-800`). Neither can be read back, but someone who guessed every other line exactly, with the second it was said and its side, could confirm the guess against `transcriptHash`; only a very short call makes that conceivable.

## Coding agents

Off by default. While on, agents reach meetings only through the running app's owner-only socket with a token (`apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/agents.rs`).

- Agents only read unless you also turn on "Let agents claim and complete action items", which is off by default and goes back off whenever you turn agents off. While it is off, the socket refuses claim and complete with a message naming that switch (`dispatch`, `agents.rs:264`; test `agents_read_only_by_default_and_are_told_which_switch_to_turn_on`). An `agents.json` saved before the switch existed reads as off. Turning agents off closes the socket and ends connections that are still open (test `turning_agents_off_ends_connections_that_are_still_open`).
- Even with the switch on, an agent can only claim an action item, mark it done with a note and a link to the work, or propose a follow-up: a recap to an email address, or a USDC payment to a payee already on your list within your per-payment cap and what's left today. A proposal is marked "Proposed by" the agent, shows its reason in place of a quote, waits for your tap, never runs on autopilot and never reaches the phone (`draft`, `agents.rs`; tests `agents_propose_payments_only_to_listed_payees_within_the_caps`, `autopilot.test.ts`, `messages.test.ts`). It can't send email, post to Slack, pay, record proofs, or change or delete notes.
- You can keep agents to chosen projects (Coding agents → "Projects agents can reach"). Meetings in other projects aren't listed, searched or readable, and a request for one is refused without naming the project (test `agents_reach_only_the_projects_the_user_chose`). Deleting a project you keep from agents doesn't open its meetings to them: they move to another project kept from agents, or to General, which is then kept from them too, as the confirm says (`projects.test.ts`). "Open in Claude Code" starts nothing for such a meeting, even with agents off (test `open_in_claude_code_says_what_is_missing`), and "Hand to an agent" offers no prompt to copy (`handoff.test.ts`: "a project kept from agents says only that, and that no prompt is offered, whatever else is off").
- "Hand to an agent" puts the action item's text, owner and due date in a prompt you copy, or, with "Open in Claude Code", in a file only you can read that Terminal passes to `claude`; the agent then sends it to its own model provider, as it does with any meeting it reads through Aftercall. Nothing is copied or started until you choose one.
- Agents see action items as you edited them, with an `edited` field (`apps/mcp/src/tools.ts:83`), and meeting start times as ISO times. They never get your notes, their salt, the notice, the cost estimate or the line key, so an agent can't make a line link (`for_agents`, above).
- The activity list (`agents-activity.json`, sealed) records each read, claim, completion and proposal, and every refused request: the time, the name the agent gives itself (not checked), the request, well-formed meeting and item ids, and whether it was allowed. Never error text or meeting content; the screen looks up titles and item text in the meetings the app already has. The newest 200 are kept (tests `the_activity_list_holds_ids_and_names_never_meeting_content`, `the_activity_list_keeps_the_newest_200`).
- Honest limits: the token sits in the agent's own config file (for example `~/.claude.json`), so other software running as you that can read that file can reach your meetings too. When Claude Code isn't connected with the current token, Open in Claude Code also writes it to a private `--mcp-config` file (0600, in a 0700 folder under the app's `agents/` folder), deleted when `claude` exits or, if Terminal was closed first, by the next Open in Claude Code once it is over 2 minutes old and its script has stopped (`open_in_claude_code`, `apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/agents.rs:1122`).

## Aftercall backend (NestJS)

### Recap email: nothing kept

- The recap (recipient, subject, body, reply-to) is held in memory for one request, checked, handed to Resend and dropped: header comment at `apps/backend/src/email/email.service.ts:5`, type `TransientRecap`.
- A recap is accepted only if it ends with the footer the app writes, found by its line "What was and wasn't checked:" (`RECAP_FOOTER_MARK`, `email.service.ts:55`; the same constant as `packages/core/src/followups/recap.ts`, checked in `email.test.ts`). The footer says only what the app knows: whether every secure-hardware check passed, whether the transcript was end-to-end encrypted, how many of the open action items in the recap the sender changed after the notes were made (marked "(edited by the sender)") and that Aftercall didn't check those changes, if there are any, that the audio is deleted once the notes are saved, the sender's notice if they logged one (as their statement), and a link to the proof or to the web page `/handling`.
- What stays: today's send count per account under a hashed key (`limitKey`, `apps/backend/src/limits.ts`), in Postgres or, without `DATABASE_URL`, in memory, for the 50-a-day limit (`sentToday`, `email.service.ts:67`), and the global `emails_sent` counter (`email.service.ts:101`, table `counters`, `apps/backend/src/stats/counters.ts:3`).
- Errors from Resend can quote the address, so they are turned into a plain `SEND_FAILED (Resend <status>)` and nothing is logged (`email.service.ts:97`).
- Tested: `apps/backend/src/email/email.test.ts:171` sends a recap through the real HTTP route with a spy Resend client, a spy database behind the counters and every Nest and console log captured, and checks that the only database write is `emails_sent`, that no log line, stored value or error contains the recipient, subject or body, and that a failed send is not even counted.
- Honest limits: the backend process does see the recap text in memory while the request runs, and the hosting provider sees the request (TLS ends at the backend). Resend stores sent mail under its own policy. If you don't want either, use **Draft in Mail**, which is the default button (`apps/desktop/src/FollowUps.tsx`, `apps/desktop/src/followUpActions.ts:61`).

### Claim links: only what the link needs

- Stored: a sha256 of the token, the owner, the amount, created and expiry times, the status and the account the payee shared: schema at `apps/backend/src/claims/claims.store.ts:61`; reasoning at `apps/backend/src/claims/claims.service.ts:11`.
- Not stored: the payee's name, a memo and the meeting title. The controller reads only `amount` from the request (`apps/backend/src/claims/claims.controller.ts:17`). The app puts the details in the link's `#fragment` (`packages/core/src/followups/claimLink.ts:27`, called from `apps/desktop/src/followUpActions.ts:251`), which browsers never send to any server; the claim page reads them in the payee's browser (`apps/web/app/claim/ClaimClient.tsx:38`). The amount shown always comes from the server, so editing the fragment can't change it.
- Older databases are migrated on start: the `payee_name`, `memo` and `meeting` columns are dropped with their contents (`claims.store.ts:76`).
- How long: links expire after 7 days (`claims.service.ts:33`). Expired and used rows are kept for the owner's list and the claim totals in `/stats`; they hold no names or meeting text.
- Tested: `apps/backend/src/claims/claims.test.ts:45` (details sent by an older app are not kept, in memory and in Postgres) and `claims.test.ts:106` (the live Postgres table has exactly the allowed columns, including after migrating an old table with data in it).
- Honest limits: the token travels in the URL path of `GET/POST /claims/link/<token>`, so a hosting provider's access log may record it until the link is used or expires.

### Phone relay: sealed boxes only

- Stored, if you pair a phone: sha256 hashes of the pairing id and of the Mac's and the phone's tokens, the sealed hello until the Mac confirms it, the latest sealed snapshot (replaced on each post) and the phone's sealed taps, with their times and the time the Mac last called: tables at `apps/backend/src/relay/relay.store.ts:129`. No key, so it can't open or forge a box.
- How long: a pairing not confirmed within 10 minutes is gone, a confirmed one 30 days after the Mac last called; a tap until the Mac acknowledges it, 24 hours at most (`PAIRING_WAIT_MS`, `PAIRING_IDLE_MS`, `ACTION_TTL_MS`, `apps/backend/src/relay/relay.service.ts:21`). Unpairing from either side deletes all of it.
- Limits are counted like the recap limit, under a hashed key: pairings and hellos per IP a day, snapshots and taps per pairing a day (`RELAY_DAILY`, `relay.service.ts:29`).
- Tested: `apps/backend/src/relay/relay.test.ts`: "nothing readable is stored: only hashes of the id and tokens, and the sealed boxes as posted", "unpairing, by either side, deletes the pairing, its snapshot and its actions", and, against Postgres, "the relay's tables have exactly the columns in the protocol".
- Honest limits: the backend sees when each side calls, the boxes' padded sizes and the callers' IP addresses (TLS ends at the backend), and it could hold back or delay boxes; the phone then says your Mac is offline, and nothing runs. The per-IP limit reads `x-vercel-forwarded-for`, then `x-real-ip`, which Vercel sets from the connection, and only without them the first `x-forwarded-for` address (`apps/backend/src/clientIp.ts`); run anywhere without a proxy that sets these, a caller can set them too.

### Sign-in and stats

- NEP-413 sign-in keeps a single-use nonce in memory until it is checked or expires (`apps/backend/src/auth/auth.service.ts:55`); sessions are signed tokens, not rows.
- `/stats` returns counts only (`apps/backend/src/stats/stats.test.ts:33`).

## A paired phone

The protocol is [docs/design/phone-protocol.md](design/phone-protocol.md).

- What the phone may see is decided by one schema: each item is built by `toPendingItem` from a follow-up and its meeting's title and start only, and `sealSnapshot` seals the parsed snapshot, so no other field can ride along (`packages/core/src/relay/messages.ts`; the Mac's snapshot builder is `buildSnapshot` in `packages/core/src/relay/mac.ts`).
- The relay sees opaque boxes, when each side calls, and the pairing id and both bearer tokens only as sha256 hashes at rest. Every action box is padded to the same size, so it can't tell an approve from a dismiss. The pairing travels in the QR code's URL fragment, which browsers never send to a server.
- Keys: the Mac's relay secret key, its relay token and the session key are one Keychain item, `phone-relay`; the pairing id, the phone's label and public key, and the counters are in `phone.json`, sealed like the other settings (`apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/phone.rs`). The app's window holds the session key in memory while it runs, because the relay's cryptography is the shared TypeScript in `packages/core/src/relay/`.
- A tap does nothing by itself: the Mac opens it, refuses a replayed, reflected or tampered box, re-checks the follow-up as it is now (`checkPhoneAction`), and only then runs the app's own Approve, Dismiss or Undo, under the same rules and caps (`handlePhoneActions` in `packages/core/src/relay/mac.ts`, called from `apps/desktop/src/phoneSync.ts`).
- Tested: `packages/core/src/relay/mac.test.ts` pairs, posts a snapshot, taps and acts through a fake relay, and checks the relay holds no name, amount, quote, label or token in readable form.
- Unpair (Settings → Your phone) deletes the pairing, its snapshot and its waiting taps on the relay, then the Keychain item and `phone.json`.

## NEAR blockchain (public, permanent)

- `record_proof(meeting_id, proof_hash, model)`: `apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/near.rs:224`. The meeting id is the recording's start time in Unix seconds. The hash covers the proof bundle; no transcript or notes are on-chain. The public Verify page reads only this from NEAR RPC (`readOnChainProof`, `packages/core/src/notes/proofLink.ts:36`); the bundle itself travels in the proof link's fragment, and a shared line in its line link's fragment.
- `pay(payee, amount, memo, followup_id, meeting_id)`: `apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/near.rs` (`pay`). `meeting_id` is sent only when the meeting's proof is on the same contract and account (`proofHere`, `apps/desktop/src/followUpActions.ts`); the contract then stores the proof's id and hash with the payment (`get_payment_proof`), both already public.
- `open_escrow(escrow_id, payee, amount, meeting_id, terms_hash, has_condition)`: `near.rs` (`open_escrow`), from `holdInEscrow` in `followUpActions.ts`. The terms hash is `dealTermsHash` (`packages/core/src/followups/deal.ts`): sha256 over the terms with 16 random bytes of salt, so it can't be used to check a guess of the quotes. The payee's `confirm_escrow` puts their own account and the same hash on chain. The memo is the fixed text `Aftercall payment` (`PAY_MEMO`, `packages/core/src/followups/boundaries.ts:11`), so nothing about the meeting goes on chain; the follow-up id is `<recording id>-<follow-up id>` (`contractFollowupId`, `packages/core/src/relay/mac.ts:80`), and the recording id is its start time in Unix seconds, so the chain shows when a paid meeting started.
