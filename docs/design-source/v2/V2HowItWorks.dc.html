How Aftercall keeps your meetings private

Nothing leaves your Mac until NEAR AI's secure hardware passes its checks, and only the checked model can read your transcript.

End-to-end encrypted summaries

The transcript is sealed on your Mac to a key bound in Qwen's verified quote, so the gateway passes on text it can't read.

Audio isn't end-to-end encryptedNEAR AI doesn't offer it for transcription. Audio goes over TLS through the attested gateway, and transcriptions aren't signed per response. The Verify sheet says so next to the Whisper checks.
  1. 1 Your Mac takes the key from a verified quote Qwen's Ed25519 key has to sit in the report data of a quote that passed every check, next to this run's nonce.
  2. 2 The transcript is sealed to that key, on your Mac Each message gets a fresh X25519 key pair. The shared secret goes through HKDF-SHA256, then XChaCha20-Poly1305 seals the text. [one-time key 32] [nonce 24] [ciphertext + tag]
  3. 3 The notes come back sealed to your Mac Qwen encrypts its reply to a key pair made for this one request. If end-to-end encryption isn't available, nothing is sent: the transcript never goes over TLS.

The limit
This stops other apps, backup and sync tools from reading your meetings. It doesn't protect a Mac account someone else has taken over.

What we can't prove

Every Verify page lists these under the checks.

  • That the transcript is accurate: Whisper can mishear names and numbers.
  • That this transcription came from the attested Whisper TEE: NEAR AI signs chat responses, not transcriptions.
  • That everyone on the call agreed to be recorded: Aftercall asks you to confirm it, and can't check it.
  • Who said what: “You” is your microphone and “Others” is the call's audio.
  • What NEAR AI keeps: attestation proves the models ran in TEEs, not what NEAR AI's gateway logs.
